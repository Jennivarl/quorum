 articles. Where is Lagos located in Nigeria? Lagos is both a city and a state in southwestern Nigeria, situated on the coast of the Bight of Benin. The city is centered on Lagos Island, within the Lagos Lagoon, positioned on the Bight of Benin in the Gulf of Guinea. Lagos State is bounded by Ogun State to the north and east, the Bight of Benin to the south, and the Republic of Benin to the west. Lagos was the federal capital of Nigeria until December 1991, when Abuja replaced it. Lagos is considered an important city in Nigeria because it is the country's largest city and one of the largest in sub-Saharan Africa. It is a major economic hub and port. The city serves as the western terminus of Nigeria’s road and railway networks, and its airport provides local and international services. Lagos is also a major educational and cultural center, hosting the University of Lagos , the National Library, Lagos city libraries, and the National Museum. Lagos has grown from a settlement of Yoruba fishermen in the late 15th century to Nigeria's largest city. In 1861, when Lagos was declared a British colony, its population was about 250,000. By the early 21st century, it had increased to approximately 8,000,000. Recent estimates show the urban agglomeration to be 13,745,000. Until 1975 it was the capital of Lagos state, and until December 1991 it was the federal capital of Nigeria. Ikeja replaced Lagos as the state capital, and Abuja replaced Lagos as the federal capital. Lagos, however, remained the unofficial seat of many government agencies. The city’s population is centred on Lagos Island, in Lagos Lagoon, on the Bight of Benin in the Gulf of Guinea . Lagos is Nigeria’s largest city and one of the largest in sub-Saharan Africa . By the late 15th century Lagos Island had been settled by Yoruba fishermen and hunters, who called it Oko. The area was dominated by the kingdom of Benin , which called it Eko, from the late 16th century to the mid-19th century. The Portuguese first landed on Lagos Island in 1472. Trade developed slowly, however, until the Portuguese were granted a series of asientos de negros —monopolies to sell slaves in the Spanish Americas—a century later. The local obas (kings) enjoyed good relations with the Portuguese, who called the island Onim (and later Lagos) and established a flourishing slave trade . ( See also transatlantic slave trade .) British attempts to suppress the slave trade culminated in 1851 in a naval attack on Lagos and the deposition of the oba.
